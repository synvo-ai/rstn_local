Cost per email processed

Only steps 3 (Understand) and 7 (Draft & verify) always call a model. Step 2 calls one only for attachments and step 4 only when the programme is unclear; steps 1, 5 and 6 are rules. That is why the unit cost is low and predictable.

Measured on the POC's formal evaluation (Round A, 28 Sep 2026): seven fixed test emails, GPT-5.6 Luna, run one at a time. Token counts are retained originals. Re-priced on 8 Oct 2026 at Luna's list price ($0.20 input, $1.20 output per 1M tokens): 4,724 × 0.20 + 1,320 × 1.20 = $0.0025 (S$0.0033), matching the documented result.

Unit cost formula for the 1-pager

cost per email = Σ stages ( calls × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) )
               + thread tokens × 2 calls × input price
               + attachment payload × calls that see it × input price   ← see "Attachments" below
               + retrieval cost per query (embeddings, search)
               + (hosting + monitoring per month) ÷ emails per month

What moves the number

  • Delivery model. In the proposed setup, model cost scales per email and is our cost. The privacy connector runs on RSTN's infrastructure (one small CPU container), so it adds nothing per email on our side; its build and support are a fixed cost. An on-prem licence turns model cost into GPU amortisation, quoted separately.
  • Fixed infrastructure at one customer. One dedicated hosting environment is est. a few hundred USD per month, the same order as PaCE's model bill (~$100–170 per month). Multi-tenancy matters for margin (hosting-and-scaling.md §2.3).
  • Data protection overhead. Masking and OCR run in the connector. Zero-retention enterprise endpoints may be priced differently from standard APIs; check when re-pricing (TODO B4).
  • Size of retrieved evidence. Real NTU pages, FAQs and policies are larger than the POC store. Capping evidence per issue keeps draft and verify in check.
  • Attachments and long threads. Under the caps, S$0.005–0.04 per email even with a 10-page scan, two images and 10 earlier messages, against a S$0.30 budget. See Attachments, long threads and the S$0.30 cap.
  • Model choice per stage. Through the model gateway, simple stages can use a cheaper model without touching business rules. In Round A, Luna was the cheapest and also scored highest in blind review (18.5 of 21).

  • Gemini 3.x (3.8 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.1 Flash-Lite gave identical counts): a fixed budget per image or page set by media_resolution, regardless of pixel size. A PDF's text layer is not billed separately and not used to save tokens: a text PDF costs the same as its scan. The default is high for images and medium for PDFs; images sent page by page fall back to high, so set medium explicitly. Medium transcribed our receipt, screenshot and 10 scanned pages correctly.
  • GPT-5.6 Luna counts 32-px patches × 1.2, up to 2,500 patches at detail: high. At auto/original it keeps the original size: a 12 MP photo would be ~14,400 tokens. Always resize (≤ 2048 px) and set the detail level. An A4 page at 150 dpi is ~2,600 tokens, ~5× Gemini medium.
  • Output is the expensive part of transcription. Transcribing 10 scanned pages produced ~5,900 output tokens (590 per page) on both 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite, at 3.75 to 6× the input price. Receipt and screenshot: 150–220 output tokens each.
  • Thinking tokens are billed as output. At thinking_level: low, 3.1 Flash-Lite added 120–1,400 per understand call; 3.8 Flash reported none. 3.8 Flash does not accept minimal.

Cost formula

cost per email = [ Σ calls ( input tokens ) × input price  +  Σ calls ( output + thinking tokens ) × output price ] × USD→SGD

input tokens   = base (Round A: 4,724 over ~4.3 calls)
               + (evidence factor − 1) × 3,096                    ← draft + verify grow with real NTU knowledge
               + thread tokens × 2                                ← understand + draft; 151 per deduplicated message
               + attachment payload × calls that see it            ← the main lever: 1 (read once) or 4 (every call)
               + 2 × 300 digest                                   ← attachment facts passed to draft + verify
               + re-checks × (evidence × 3,096 + thread + 600)     ← staff change re-runs draft + verify
output tokens  = base (1,320) + 260 with attachments + transcription (590 per page, if the model transcribes)
               + re-checks × 716
attachment payload = text pages × 620 (capped 2,000 per attachment)       ← text layer or OCR in the connector
                   | pages × image tokens per page  + images × image tokens  ← sent to the model as images

Conclusion. Every realistic case costs S$0.003–0.04 at today's prices, roughly 8–90× under the S$0.30 cap. The proposed path (text layer or OCR in the connector, 2,000-token cap per attachment, attachment read once, deduplicated thread) stays under S$0.08 even on 3.8 Flash at 2027 prices with 3× evidence and a staff re-check. Only the deliberately naive worst case on 3.8 Flash after the 2027 price rise reaches the cap. The guarantee therefore comes from four engine rules, not from the model price: (1) cap pages (10) and text kept per attachment (2,000 tokens); (2) attachments seen by one call only, then a short digest; (3) deduplicate quoted thread history; (4) resize images and set the resolution explicitly. Add a per-email token budget check in the engine that degrades to Manual handling rather than overspend (TODO B39).

One made-up 900×1200 bank-transfer receipt; cold model load adds ~10–15 s (OCR) to ~2 min (27B vision). GLM-OCR text was correct apart from one masked digit. Classic OCR not yet measured (TODO B33). At 10 pages, GLM-OCR on CPU would take ~7.5 minutes per attachment: too slow for the connector, so classic or ONNX OCR by default.

If OCR runs on our side instead

  • Hosted-engine-only fallback (RSTN cannot run the connector): OCR moves to our cloud. Classic OCR is est. under $0.0001 per page of CPU time. GLM-OCR on CPU is 4 vCPU × 45 s ≈ 0.05 vCPU-hours, est. ~$0.002 per page.
  • Model transcription instead of OCR: ~S$0.004 (Flash-Lite) to ~S$0.03 (3.8 Flash) per 10 pages, mostly output tokens. Still far under the cap, but 3–10× the proposed path.
  • GPU makes GLM-OCR ~50× faster, but a GPU node is a fixed cost of thousands of USD per month (hosting-and-scaling.md §2.4). At PaCE volume it would sit idle; worth it only when shared across customers or required on-prem.
  • Connector sizing follows the OCR choice: classic or ONNX OCR keeps "2–4 vCPU, 8 GB".
